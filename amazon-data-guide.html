How to download your Amazon order history

Amazon will hand you every order you have ever placed, going back to the day you opened the account. It is free, it takes about four clicks, and most people have no idea it exists.

No purchase needed. This guide is free.

The four steps

Requesting the file

1

Go to Amazon's data request page

Sign in to Amazon, then open Account & Lists → Your Account → Data and Privacy, and look for Request Your Information. If you would rather go straight there, it lives at amazon.com/hz/privacy-central/data-requests/preview.html.

Do this on a computer if you can. The page exists on mobile, but the menus are buried several taps deeper.

2

Choose "Your Orders", not "Request All Your Data"

The dropdown offers a long list of categories. Pick the orders category and submit.

You can request everything, but "all your data" pulls in your Kindle highlights, Alexa recordings, browsing history and a great deal else. It takes far longer to prepare, arrives as a much larger download, and none of the extra is used here.

3

Confirm the request from your email

Amazon sends a confirmation email within a few minutes. Your request does not start until you click the link inside it.

Don't skip this

This is where nearly everyone gets stuck. The confirmation email looks like a receipt, as though the request is already running, so it gets skimmed and left. It isn't a receipt. It's a switch. If your data never turns up, this is almost always why: check your inbox, and check spam and promotions too.

4

Wait for the download link, then save the file

Amazon usually takes a few hours to a day or so, though it can stretch longer if you have a decade of orders. When it is ready you get a second email with a download link.

The link expires, so download it when you see it rather than leaving it for the weekend. What lands is a .zip file. Save it somewhere you will find again. You do not need to unzip it to know what is inside, but you will want the CSVs out of it.

What arrives

What's inside the zip

Amazon's export is generous to the point of being confusing. You get a folder of CSVs, most of which you will never open. These are the ones that matter:

Your order history Needed

One row per item, not per order, with the date, the product title, the price, the quantity and the shipping address. This is the file that becomes your dashboard.

Your refunds and returns Optional

Everything you sent back. Add it and your totals become net spend rather than gross: what you actually kept, not just what you ordered. Worth including if it's there.

Everything else

Digital orders, subscriptions, gift card activity, address book exports and more. Interesting to poke at, but nothing here needs them.

Amazon has renamed these files more than once and the exact wording varies by account and region, so match on what the file contains rather than on an exact filename. If you open one and it has a row per item with a date and a price, that's the one.

Questions

Things people ask at this point

Is it safe to request this?

Yes. You are asking Amazon for a copy of your own records, which they are required to provide. It is the same right that lets you ask any company what they hold on you. Nothing changes on your account, and nobody is notified.

How far back does it go?

Usually to the day you opened the account. People who have been on Amazon since the 2000s routinely get fifteen-plus years of orders back. That length of history is the whole reason this is interesting: one year tells you what you bought, fifteen tells you how you changed.

I requested it and nothing arrived.

Ninety percent of the time it's step 3: the confirmation email was never clicked. Check spam and promotions. If you did confirm and it has been more than a few days, just submit the request again; there is no penalty for asking twice.
